Help & Terms

What is POTS?

POTS (Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome) is a way of saying your body has a hard time adjusting when you stand up. Your heart rate jumps, making you dizzy or super tired. Tracking your daily habits helps you learn what triggers a flare-up so you can feel better!

Dizziness / Lightheadedness

That woozy, spinning feeling you get when standing up or sitting for too long.

Racing Heart (Tachycardia)

When your heart beats super fast or pounds heavily in your chest, even though you are just standing or resting.

Tiredness (Fatigue)

Feeling completely out of energy, even if you slept well.

Brain Fog

When your head feels cloudy or fuzzy. It makes it hard to focus, think, or remember things.

Nausea

Feeling sick to your stomach, like you might throw up.

Fainting (Syncope)

Actually passing out or feeling like you were just about to collapse.

Water Intake (Hydration)

Drinking plenty of fluids helps keep your blood volume up, stopping your heart from racing. We track this in ounces (oz) now. US doctors often recommend 64 to 100+ oz a day for POTS!

Sleep Time

How many hours of sleep you got. Lack of sleep is a common trigger for POTS flare-ups.

Time Spent Standing

Total minutes you spent standing up. Standing puts stress on your body, so keeping track of it helps you find your limits.

Air Pressure (Barometer)

Weather changes can drop air pressure and make POTS symptoms worse!

Period Cycle Day

Hormone drops during your period can trigger POTS flares. Log which day of your cycle you're on to spot links.
